# Script: "This Border Moves Every Few Years"

**Format reference:** GeoGlobeTales-style short (map-animation + VO, 60s, YouTube Shorts/TikTok) — Day 25 of 30-day lineup
**Topic:** The shifting Rio Grande border (US-Mexico) and the Chamizal dispute, El Paso/Ciudad Juárez
**Target length:** ~60 seconds (6 beats x 10s)
**Tone:** Calm, confident, deadpan-surprised narrator. Short declarative sentences.

**VO word-count discipline:** Lines pre-tuned to ~20-25 words each (beat 6 runs longer for the CTA), targeting a natural ~8-10s read per beat. Narrator is Google TTS — normal digit formatting is fine, no need to spell out numbers.

---

## TITLE OPTIONS
1. "This Border Moves Every Few Years 🌊" (primary)
2. "A River Keeps Redrawing This International Border"
3. "The US-Mexico Border That Refused to Stay Put"

---

## SCRIPT (6 x 10s beats)

**Beat 1 — HOOK (0:00–0:10)**
VO: "Most borders are fixed lines on a map. This one moves — because it's not a line, it's a river, and rivers don't sit still."

*VISUAL: Push-in on a winding blue river between green land, the river's course subtly shifting as the camera settles.*
*ON-SCREEN TEXT: "This border moves."*

**Beat 2 — SETUP (0:10–0:20)**
VO: "The Rio Grande has marked the US-Mexico border for over 150 years, but its channel keeps shifting, sometimes overnight during a flood."

*VISUAL: Map zoom along the river's path from the Gulf of Mexico up to El Paso, US and Mexico gold-outlined on either bank.*
*ON-SCREEN TEXT: "A river as a border."*

**Beat 3 — ESCALATION 1 (0:20–0:30)**
VO: "In 1864, a flood pushed the river south at El Paso, stranding a patch of Mexican land called El Chamizal on the American side."

*VISUAL: Tight zoom on El Paso/Ciudad Juárez, old river course fading and a new course animating in further south, a small parcel highlighted between them.*
*ON-SCREEN TEXT: "The 1864 flood."*

**Beat 4 — ESCALATION 2 (0:30–0:40)**
VO: "For nearly a century, the US and Mexico argued over who actually owned it — because the map said one thing, and the river said another."

*VISUAL: Two dashed lines overlapping across the same stretch of river, labeled "map" and "river," flickering between each other.*
*ON-SCREEN TEXT: "A century-long dispute."*

**Beat 5 — TWIST / CLIMAX (0:40–0:50)**
VO: "In 1963, both countries gave up and rebuilt the river itself — concrete-lining a new fixed channel just to make the border hold still."

*VISUAL: A solid gold channel line animating into place over the old wandering course, locking the border into a single straight path.*
*ON-SCREEN TEXT: "Concrete fixed the border."*

**Beat 6 — BUTTON / CTA (0:50–1:00)**
VO: "A river redrew a national border for a hundred years, until engineers finally poured concrete just to make geography behave. Follow for more places on Earth that shouldn't exist, but do."

*VISUAL: Pull back to the full US-Mexico border from the Gulf to El Paso, river glowing gold, freeze on title card.*
*ON-SCREEN TEXT: "Follow for more 🌍"*

## VISUAL STYLE DIRECTION (continue established look)
- Same blue-water / green-land base palette; the river itself carries the gold accent (via animated lines) rather than a single highlighted country, since the story is about the boundary moving, not about either nation individually.
- US and Mexico both lightly gold-outlined as the two countries the river separates, keeping the classic atlas look consistent with the rest of the series.

## PRODUCTION NOTES
- Local zero-cost pipeline: D3 map render + Google TTS narration + burned captions, same locked style key as every other video in the lineup — no per-video visual changes.
- `highlight: ["US", "MX"]` throughout; the moving river itself is carried by animated `lines`, since no static country polygon can show a channel that shifts.
